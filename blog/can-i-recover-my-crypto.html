Can I Recover My Crypto? An Honest Guide to What's Actually Possible

ReclaimGuard Legal Team 11 July 2026 6 min read

If you have lost cryptocurrency to a scam, the first question is almost always the same: “Can I actually get my crypto back?” You deserve an honest answer, not false hope and not a flat “no.” The truth is that recovery is sometimes possible, and whether it is depends on a handful of specific factors. This guide explains exactly what those are, so you can understand your real situation.

The honest answer: sometimes

Cryptocurrency recovery is realistic in some cases and very difficult in others. What separates the two is not luck it is a few concrete factors about where your funds went and how quickly you act. Anyone who tells you recovery is guaranteed is not being honest with you.

Recovery is never guaranteed. But “not guaranteed” is very different from “impossible” and the only way to know your real chances is to have the case assessed.

What actually determines whether you can recover crypto

Four factors matter more than anything else:

  • Where the funds ended up. Stolen crypto is often moved to a regulated exchange to be cashed out. Regulated exchanges must collect identity (KYC) data and can freeze accounts when presented with proper legal requests. If your funds reached one and are still there, recovery potential is real.
  • How fast you act. Scammers move money quickly, sometimes within hours. The sooner tracing and legal freeze requests begin, the better.
  • Whether the trail is clean. If funds passed through a mixer or tumbler, or were split across dozens of wallets, tracing is harder (though not always impossible).
  • Whether funds have already been withdrawn. Once cashed out to a bank account and moved on, recovery becomes significantly harder.

Can Bitcoin really be traced?

Yes. The blockchain is a permanent, public record of every transaction. Using blockchain analytics tools, investigators can follow funds from wallet to wallet and frequently identify the exchange that received them. But it is important to understand a key point: tracing shows where the money went it does not, by itself, return it. Tracing has to be combined with legal action a formal freeze request to the exchange, a regulatory complaint, or court proceedings to actually recover funds.

When recovery is genuinely unlikely

We would rather be straight with you than take a hopeless case. Recovery is much less likely when:

  • Funds were laundered through mixers a long time ago and cashed out;
  • The crypto was sent directly from your self-custody wallet and long since withdrawn from every exchange in the chain;
  • Many months or years have passed with no trace preserved.

Even then, a proper assessment is worthwhile because the answer depends on the specific on-chain trail, not a general rule.

What to do right now to protect your chances

  1. Stop all contact and payments. Never send “fees,” “taxes,” or “unlock” payments to recover funds that is how scammers extract more money.
  2. Preserve everything. Save transaction IDs (TXIDs), wallet addresses, the platform URL, screenshots, and all messages.
  3. Report it. File with your national fraud body (Action Fraud in the UK, the FBI's IC3 in the US) and your bank if a card or transfer was involved.
  4. Get the trail assessed quickly while the funds may still be traceable and freezable.

Beware of recovery scams. If someone contacts you promising to recover your crypto, guarantees results, or demands a large upfront payment, walk away. Preying on scam victims a second time is one of the most common frauds. Read our guide on how to spot a crypto recovery scam.

Frequently asked questions

Can I recover my crypto after a scam?

Sometimes. It is realistic when funds can be traced to a regulated exchange that still holds them and there is a legal route to freeze the account. It is much harder after mixing, withdrawal, or long delays. No honest firm guarantees recovery.

How long do I have?

Act immediately your best window is the first hours and days. Older cases are not automatically hopeless, but time works against you.

Do recovery services really work?

Legitimate, regulated firms can help in genuine cases; no honest one promises guaranteed results. Guarantees and large upfront demands are hallmarks of secondary scams.
