Fake Trading Platform Won't Let You Withdraw? Here's What to Do

ReclaimGuard Legal Team 11 July 2026 6 min read

You invested through what looked like a legitimate trading or crypto platform, your balance grew nicely and now that you want to withdraw, you can't. Suddenly there are “taxes” to pay, “fees” to clear, or a “verification deposit” required first. If this is happening to you, please read carefully: this is one of the most common scams in the world, and understanding it now can stop you losing more.

Do not pay the withdrawal fee. No legitimate platform ever requires you to send more money to release your own balance. Paying will not get your money out it only deepens the loss.

Why you can't withdraw: how the scam works

Fake brokers and investment platforms follow a predictable script:

  • You deposit a modest amount and are shown impressive, steadily rising “profits.” These numbers are entirely fabricated the platform is just a dashboard.
  • A friendly “account manager” encourages you to deposit more to unlock bigger returns.
  • When you try to withdraw, obstacles appear: a “withdrawal tax,” “capital gains fee,” “anti-money-laundering deposit,” or “account upgrade” is suddenly required.
  • Each fee you pay is followed by another. The goal is to extract as much as possible before you realise the balance was never real.

Warning signs you're dealing with a fake platform

  • You must pay fees or “taxes” before withdrawing;
  • The broker isn't authorised by a real regulator (FCA in the UK, BaFin in Germany, SEC/CFTC in the US) always check the regulator's own register, not the platform's claims;
  • You were approached via social media, a dating app, WhatsApp, or a “tip” from a new online friend;
  • Returns are unusually high, fast, and consistent;
  • Pressure and urgency whenever you hesitate.

What to do right now

  1. Stop paying and stop depositing. Do not send another penny, however convincing the “one last fee” sounds.
  2. Screenshot everything: the platform, your “balance,” the fee demands, and all chats with the “account manager.”
  3. Note how you funded the account card, bank transfer, or crypto and the dates and amounts.
  4. Contact your bank if you paid by card or transfer, and report to your national fraud body.
  5. Get the case assessed so tracing or disputes can begin while the trail is fresh.

Can you get the money back?

Sometimes yes, and it depends largely on how you paid:

  • Card: a chargeback (and Section 75 in the UK) may reverse the payment;
  • Bank transfer: a recall and an APP fraud reimbursement claim may apply;
  • Crypto: funds can be traced and pursued through legal freeze requests to the receiving exchange see can I recover my crypto?

For the full step-by-step by payment method, read how to get your money back after being scammed.

Watch for the follow-up scam. Victims of fake platforms are often contacted by fake “recovery agents” who guarantee to get the money back for an upfront fee. That's a second scam. See how to spot a recovery scam.
